What a key point is One thing you want the viewer to take away, written as a pair: the statement, and a short phrase that must appear in a subtitle. For example, the statement "Mumbai's suburban railway carries about 7 million passengers a day" paired with the phrase 7 million.

It is a pair because a machine cannot measure whether something "came across". The check (7.4.6) searches the finished video's subtitles for 7 million; if the phrase is there, that key point counts as shown. The statement is what the model is given when it writes the storyboard.

Key points are decided by you, not by the agent. Three or more, with a source noted for any that contain a fact (7.3.1).

7.1Breaking video production into a flow

"Make a video" is not one job. Split, it looks like this.

StepWhoWhat is decided
1　Key pointsHumanThe three things the video says. Not the agent's call
2　Storyboard and scriptAgent (27B)6–8 cuts. For each: the generation prompt, seconds, subtitle. The narration
3　ApprovalHumanRead every prompt. Stop before any material is made
4　MaterialHuman + outside toolsGenerate each cut within the rules in 7.2, put it in assets/, log the source
5　EditingAgent (27B + tools)Looks at the clips that came back and decides order, trims, transitions and subtitle timing, calling the tools itself
6　CheckMachineLength, key points in subtitles, source log, narration length
7　Release decisionHumanWatch it. Approve or stop

The model is called in steps 2 and 5, but they are different kinds of work. Step 2 writes: text in, JSON out, no tools. Step 5 orchestrates: it looks at the material and decides for itself which tools to call, in what order, with what values. It is the tool-using agent from Part 6, pointed at a different job.

Step 4 stays in human hands because the means of making material lie outside the agent. Wan2.1 on Colab takes minutes per clip, generation services spend credits, and shooting it yourself means going there. Whichever way, one run gives a few seconds, and making several of them is human logistics. An agent left to loop over it burns time and credits without judgement. A person makes the cuts by the means in 7.2 while the agent waits.

Fixed procedure versus orchestration

There are two ways to put tools together.

Fixed procedure (a script)Orchestration (an agent)
Who decides the orderWhoever wrote it. Same every timeThe model. Depends on the situation
SuitsInputs of a known shape, where the same result is requiredInputs that vary, go missing, or come in surplus
In this partThe check (6). The tools themselves: normalize, join, and so onEditing (5). How the tools are used
RiskBreaks on unexpected inputHard to see what happened. Hence the log

The clips come from three services, differ in length and orientation, and some cuts failed to generate. "cut3 is missing, use a different part of cut1"; "the portrait one, keep short": these are decided on the spot, and a fixed procedure cannot list them in advance. So editing is orchestrated, and it is wrapped on both sides by a fixed check and a human decision. A zone of free judgement, enclosed by layers that do not judge. That is the shape carried over from Part 6.

What one generation cannot make

A video model returns two to five seconds per run. The next run knows nothing about the last one. A sequence in time, like a train slowing to a stop, doors opening, the view changing, cannot come out of one run. You split it into cuts, decide order and length, and join them. That is the flow. You will see it as soon as you start making material.

7.2Rules for material

Material can be made with anything: a generation service, your own camera, a stock site. The instructor does not name a service and does not hold an account for you. The rules are not about tools. They are these three conditions.

The rights are checkedCopyright, likeness, trademarks. The items in the warning box below, checked clip by clip

Looking at the clips, joining them, subtitling and checking the result is the job of your modified production agent.

What "four or more different clips" can be

"Different" means not a regeneration of the same prompt. They need not all be generated video. Mixing kinds is better: it gives the editing agent something to decide.

KindExamplesOrigin
Generated videoA local train at dawn, Marine Drive from above, steam over a food stallGeneration service
Video you shotYour commute, a station platform, rain from your window, the campus courtyardYour camera
Photos you tookFestival decorations, a meal on the table. Turn each into a few seconds of slow zoom before add_to_timelineYour camera
A generated image, animatedOne generated picture with a slow pan, a few seconds longGeneration service
Stock footageWaves, monsoon clouds, an aerial night view of a cityStock site
Screen recording or diagramA map zooming in to Mumbai, a simple diagram of your own. Check the map service's termsMade by you
Text-only cutOne line on a black background: the opening title or the closing sentenceMade by you

For example: two generated clips, one video you shot, one of your photos animated, one closing text cut. That is five. Different kinds come in different lengths and orientations. Looking at them and making them fit is the editing agent's job (7.1).

Copyright and likeness are your responsibility Before placing a clip, check the following for each one. The check only sees whether a source line exists. Whether the content may be used is something a machine cannot tell.
  • Is credit required? Record all sources in SOURCES.txt. In addition, if the license or the service requires attribution in the published work (CC BY and the like), the required credit must also appear in the video, for example as a credit cut at the end (Video: photographer / site). SOURCES.txt is seen only by the instructor and does not satisfy that condition on its own. If credit is required and you cannot give it, do not use the clip
  • Faces. If passers-by or friends are recognisable in video you shot, either have their consent or frame so faces cannot be identified. Keep "no real people's faces" in your generation prompts
  • Logos, signs, characters, film posters. They appear naturally in street footage. Avoid cuts where they fill the frame. Never use film or music material itself
  • Sound. The starter's only audio is the narration; the standard agent cannot mix in music. Music is optional and requires modifying video_tools.py (Appendix D). If you do, use only a track whose commercial-use and credit terms you have checked, and add a music | … line to SOURCES.txt
  • Maps and tourism-site images. Screenshots only within what that service's terms allow
When in doubt, leave it out, or ask the instructor at the release decision (7.5). "An AI made it" is not a reason. Generation services have terms about commercial use and credit too.

The source log, SOURCES.txt

In assets/SOURCES.txt, one or more lines per cut. The first line gives origin, prompt and date. If there is more to record (credit, license, how faces were handled), add as many further lines starting with the same cutN as you need. Nothing has to be squeezed onto one line.

cut1 | Colab Wan2.1 (course notebook) | slow pan over a Mumbai suburban train at dawn, wide shot | 2026-09-10
cut2 | Kling | Marine Drive at night from above, streetlights curving along the bay | 2026-09-10
cut3 | own video | - | 2026-09-08
cut4 | Pexels: https://www.pexels.com/video/... | - | 2026-09-10
cut5 | own photo (slow zoom) | - | 2026-09-08
cut6 | Pixabay: https://pixabay.com/videos/... | - | 2026-09-10
cut6 | credit | "Video by NAME from Pixabay", shown in the final cut
cut6 | license | Pixabay Content License
cut3 | note | passers-by not identifiable, shot from behind

For material that requires credit, write the required wording and where in the video it appears on a credit line, as in cut6 above. The check only looks for a line starting with that cutN; how many lines there are does not matter.

The check (7.4.6) fails any cut without a line here. Material you cannot log is material you cannot use.

7.3.2Required changes

Everyone makes these four changes. Rewriting the theme is not enough: changing at least one thing the agent does is required.

ChangeWhere
1Theme and audienceTHEME, AUDIENCE
2Three or more key points for your videoKEY_POINTS
3Two or more success conditions added or changedcheck and the constants it reads
4One or more new agent behavioursA node, an edge, a tool, or EDITOR_SYSTEM

Examples for 3 (success conditions): length inside your own range (for example 40–50 s); every required subtitle present; a maximum length for a subtitle line; no clip used twice; a minimum word count for the narration; no forbidden phrase or logo. Re-adding a check the starter already has (30–60 s, four clips, key points, sources, narration length) does not count.

Examples for 4 (agent behaviour): a new human approval (for example "continue / stop" right after the clip list); a way to handle missing clips (tell the human which cuts are missing and wait again); a restriction on the order of tool calls; the agent saving a reason per cut into run/; a pre-release checklist; different handling when the revision limit is reached.

Records are enforced by the program Every run of the starter writes run/EXECUTION_TRACE.jsonl: each storyboard version (also saved as run/STORYBOARD_V1.json …), each human approval or revision with its reason, the clip list, every tool call, every check result and the release decision, one JSON line per event in the order they happened. The model is not asked to record anything; the Python in each node appends the line the moment the thing happens. Do the same when you add behaviour: a new approval or check gets one trace("...") line into the same file. This is the Part 6 idea again: surround the part that is free to decide with layers that do not decide.

    draft ──► approve_storyboard ──► wait_for_clips ──► edit ──► check ──pass──► review ──► END

7.4.3Plan and approval

def draft(state: State) -> State:
    feedback = state.get("feedback", "")
    min_total = MIN_SECONDS + FADE * (NUM_CUTS - 1)
    prompt = f"""You are planning a PR video of at most {MAX_SECONDS} seconds.
The cuts will be joined with {FADE}-second crossfades, which shorten the video, so the cut lengths
must add up to at least {min_total:.0f} seconds and at most {MAX_SECONDS} seconds.
Theme: {THEME}
Audience: {AUDIENCE}
Key points that MUST appear, each in at least one subtitle:
{chr(10).join('- ' + k for k, _ in KEY_POINTS)}

Return ONLY a JSON object with:
- "storyboard": a list of exactly {NUM_CUTS} cuts. Each cut: {{"cut": n, "seconds": 5-10, "prompt": "a text-to-video prompt for this shot, one sentence, no real people's faces, no logos", "subtitle": "one short line shown on screen"}}
- "narration": the voice-over for the whole video, at most {int(MAX_SECONDS * 2.3)} words, plain English.
{('Feedback from the human reviewer, apply it: ' + feedback) if feedback else ''}"""
    data = None
    for attempt in range(1, 3):                       # the model's JSON is not always valid: try twice
        text = model.invoke(prompt).content
        try:
            m = re.search(r"\{.*\}", text, re.S)
            if not m:
                raise ValueError("no JSON object in the reply")
            data = json.loads(m.group(0))
            if not isinstance(data.get("storyboard"), list) or "narration" not in data:
                raise ValueError("JSON has no 'storyboard' list or no 'narration'")
            break
        except Exception as e:
            trace("storyboard_error", attempt=attempt, error=str(e), model_output=text[:2000])
            print(f"  [draft] attempt {attempt}: the model's reply was not a valid storyboard ({e})")
    if data is None:
        raise RuntimeError("The model did not return a valid storyboard JSON after 2 attempts. "
                           "Run the agent again; the replies are in run/EXECUTION_TRACE.jsonl.")
    version = state.get("storyboard_version", 0) + 1
    trace("storyboard_generated", version=version, feedback_applied=feedback,
          storyboard=data["storyboard"], narration=data["narration"])
    (RUN_DIR / f"STORYBOARD_V{version}.json").write_text(
        json.dumps(data, indent=2, ensure_ascii=False), encoding="utf-8")
    return {**state, "storyboard": data["storyboard"], "narration": data["narration"],
            "storyboard_version": version, "feedback": ""}


def approve_storyboard(state: State) -> State:
    """Interrupt 1: the human reads every prompt before any clip is generated."""
    lines = [f"cut{c['cut']}  {c['seconds']}s  subtitle: {c['subtitle']}\n        prompt: {c['prompt']}"
             for c in state["storyboard"]]
    total = sum(c["seconds"] for c in state["storyboard"])
    answer = interrupt({
        "storyboard": "\n".join(lines),
        "narration": state["narration"],
        "total_seconds": f"{total} (about {total - FADE * (len(lines) - 1):.0f}s after crossfades; "
                         f"the video must be {MIN_SECONDS}-{MAX_SECONDS}s)",
        "question": "Approve this storyboard? (approve / revise / stop)",
    })
    trace("human_decision", stage="storyboard", version=state.get("storyboard_version"),
          decision=answer["decision"], feedback=answer.get("feedback", ""))
    return {**state, "decision": answer["decision"], "feedback": answer.get("feedback", "")}

draft is the node that writes. It returns the storyboard and script as JSON and touches no video file. The model's reply is sometimes broken JSON, so it is tried twice; if both fail, the run stops and the replies are in run/EXECUTION_TRACE.jsonl. Python then saves the JSON as run/STORYBOARD_V1.json and records it with trace(); the model is never asked to save anything. The run stops at approve_storyboard, and you read every prompt. What to look for:

  • Do the three key points appear in some cut's subtitle?
  • Do any prompts contain faces, celebrities or logos (7.2)?
  • Do the seconds add up to enough? Clips are joined with 0.6 s crossfades, so the finished length is the total minus 0.6 × (cuts − 1): six cuts need a total of at least 33 s to reach 30 s. The approval screen shows this as total_seconds. If it is off, send it back with revise

If something is off, type revise and one line of what should change. The model rewrites with your feedback.

7.4.4Waiting for material

def wait_for_clips(state: State) -> State:
    """Interrupt 2: the human generates the clips with the allowed tools and puts them in assets/."""
    expected = [f"assets/cut{c['cut']}.mp4" for c in state["storyboard"]]
    answer = interrupt({
        "instruction": "Generate each cut with the allowed tools, save them as the files below, "
                       "and write at least one line per cut in assets/SOURCES.txt. Missing cuts are fine; "
                       "the editor will work with what is there. Then type: done",
        "files": expected,
    })
    trace("human_decision", stage="clips", decision=answer["decision"])
    return {**state, "decision": answer["decision"]}

While the run waits, you make each cut within the conditions in 7.2, save it as assets/cut1.mp4 …, and log each one in SOURCES.txt (7.2). They do not all have to be there. The editing agent builds with what exists, knowing what is missing. Type done to continue. With no clip at all in assets/, done is not accepted.

7.4.5Editing: orchestration

The tools handed to the editing agent. Each is small, makes no judgement, and logs every call.

@tool
def list_assets() -> str:
    """List the clips in assets/ with their length in seconds, width and height, and whether SOURCES.txt has a line for them."""
    sources = vt.read_sources()
    rows = []
    for p in sorted(vt.ASSETS.glob("cut*.mp4")):
        n = int(p.stem[3:])
        w, h = vt.probe_size(p)
        rows.append({"cut": n, "file": p.name, "seconds": round(vt.probe_duration(p), 1),
                     "width": w, "height": h, "has_source": n in sources})
    _log(f"list_assets -> {len(rows)} clips")
    return json.dumps(rows)


@tool
def contact_sheet() -> str:
    """Make one image with a frame from each clip (first, middle, last) so you can see what each cut shows. Returns the image path."""
    paths = sorted(vt.ASSETS.glob("cut*.mp4"))
    if not paths:
        _log("contact_sheet -> ERROR: no clips in assets/")
        return "ERROR: No clips were found in assets/. Add at least four clips (cut1.mp4, cut2.mp4, ...)."
    vt.BUILD.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
    tiles = []
    for p in paths:
        d = vt.probe_duration(p)
        for k, t in enumerate((0.2, d / 2, max(0.2, d - 0.3))):
            out = vt.BUILD / f"sheet_{p.stem}_{k}.png"
            vt.frame_at(p, t, out)
            tiles.append(Image.open(out).resize((320, 180)))
    cols = 3
    rows = (len(tiles) + cols - 1) // cols
    sheet = Image.new("RGB", (320 * cols, 180 * rows), "black")
    for i, im in enumerate(tiles):
        sheet.paste(im, ((i % cols) * 320, (i // cols) * 180))
    out = vt.BUILD / "contact_sheet.png"
    sheet.save(out)
    _log("contact_sheet -> build/contact_sheet.png")
    return str(out)


@tool
def add_to_timeline(cut: int, seconds: float, start: float = 0.0) -> str:
    """Append a clip to the timeline: cut number, how many seconds to use, and where in the clip to start (seconds). Clips are normalized to 1280x720 here."""
    src = vt.ASSETS / f"cut{cut}.mp4"
    if not src.exists():
        return f"ERROR: {src.name} does not exist"
    dst = vt.BUILD / f"t{len(_timeline) + 1}_cut{cut}.mp4"
    vt.normalize_clip(src, dst, seconds, start=start)
    _timeline.append({"cut": cut, "seconds": seconds, "start": start, "file": str(dst)})
    _log(f"add_to_timeline(cut={cut}, seconds={seconds}, start={start})")
    return f"timeline now has {len(_timeline)} clips, total {sum(c['seconds'] for c in _timeline):.1f}s"


@tool
def clear_timeline() -> str:
    """Empty the timeline to start the order again."""
    _timeline.clear()
    _log("clear_timeline")
    return "timeline cleared"


@tool
def join_timeline(fade: float = 0.6) -> str:
    """Join the timeline clips in order with a crossfade of `fade` seconds between each pair. Writes build/joined.mp4 and returns its length."""
    if not _timeline:
        return "ERROR: timeline is empty"
    vt.concat_with_crossfade([c["file"] for c in _timeline], vt.BUILD / "joined.mp4", fade=fade)
    d = vt.probe_duration(vt.BUILD / "joined.mp4")
    _log(f"join_timeline(fade={fade}) -> {d:.1f}s")
    return f"joined.mp4 is {d:.1f}s"


@tool
def add_subtitles(subtitles_json: str) -> str:
    """Burn subtitles onto build/joined.mp4. subtitles_json is a JSON list of {"start": seconds, "end": seconds, "text": "..."}. Writes build/subtitled.mp4."""
    subs = json.loads(subtitles_json)
    LAST_SUBTITLES[:] = [s["text"] for s in subs]
    vt.burn_subtitles(vt.BUILD / "joined.mp4", vt.BUILD / "subtitled.mp4", subs)
    _log(f"add_subtitles({len(subs)} lines)")
    return f"{len(subs)} subtitles burned"


@tool
def add_narration(text: str) -> str:
    """Turn the narration text into speech and put it under build/subtitled.mp4. Writes output.mp4 and returns the audio and video lengths."""
    audio_len = vt.narrate(text, vt.BUILD / "narration.mp3")
    vt.mux_audio(vt.BUILD / "subtitled.mp4", vt.BUILD / "narration.mp3", vt.PROJECT_ROOT / "output.mp4")
    video_len = vt.probe_duration(vt.PROJECT_ROOT / "output.mp4")
    _log(f"add_narration({len(text.split())} words) -> audio {audio_len:.1f}s, video {video_len:.1f}s")
    return f"output.mp4 written. audio {audio_len:.1f}s, video {video_len:.1f}s"


# list_assets and contact_sheet are run by the edit node BEFORE the agent starts (their results go into
# its first message), so the agent itself only gets the editing tools.
EDIT_TOOLS = [add_to_timeline, clear_timeline, join_timeline, add_subtitles, add_narration]

The agent itself is the same create_react_agent as in Part 6. What differs is the tools, the instructions, and that the first message contains the clip list and an image. list_assets and contact_sheet are not tools the agent chooses to call; they are fixed pre-processing run by the edit node's Python. Their results (length, size and source status of each cut, plus a sheet of the first, middle and last frame of every cut) go into the first message, and the agent calls only the editing tools. The 27B on the shared server reads images, so it can look before it decides.

EDITOR_SYSTEM = f"""You are the editor of a short PR video. You have tools to build the video.
Work like this:
1. Your first message contains the list of available clips (with lengths, sizes and whether each has a
   source line) and a contact sheet showing the first, middle and last frame of every clip. Look at
   them before deciding anything.
2. Decide the order and the length of each clip. Follow the storyboard where the clips allow it;
   if a clip is missing, too short, or does not show what its prompt asked for, say so and adapt
   (reorder, reuse a good clip for two beats, drop a cut).
3. Build the timeline with add_to_timeline, then join_timeline, then add_subtitles, then add_narration.
4. The finished video must be between {MIN_SECONDS} and {MAX_SECONDS} seconds and use at least
   {MIN_CLIPS} different clips. Joins take {FADE}s each, so plan the clip lengths accordingly.
   Each of these phrases must appear in some subtitle: {', '.join(repr(w) for _, w in KEY_POINTS)}.
   The narration must not be longer than the video: cut words before you cut clips.
5. Finish with a short summary of the decisions you made and why, especially where you departed from the storyboard.
Never invent clip numbers that are not in the clip list."""

editor = create_react_agent(model, et.EDIT_TOOLS, prompt=EDITOR_SYSTEM)


def edit(state: State) -> State:
    rnd = state.get("edit_rounds", 0) + 1
    et._timeline.clear()                      # the timeline starts empty each round ...
    et.LOG.append(f"===== EDIT ROUND {rnd} =====")   # ... but the log keeps every round
    # Fixed pre-processing, done by Python, not chosen by the model: list the clips and make the sheet.
    assets = et.list_assets.invoke({})
    sheet = et.contact_sheet.invoke({})
    trace("assets_detected", round=rnd, assets=json.loads(assets))
    brief = {"storyboard": state["storyboard"], "narration": state["narration"]}
    text = ("Storyboard and narration as approved:\n" + json.dumps(brief, indent=1)
            + "\n\nClips actually available (from list_assets):\n" + assets)
    if state.get("feedback"):
        text += "\n\nThe last attempt failed the check. Fix these:\n" + state["feedback"]
    msg = HumanMessage(content=[{"type": "text", "text": text}, et.image_message(sheet)])
    result = editor.invoke({"messages": [msg]}, {"recursion_limit": 40})
    summary = result["messages"][-1].content
    et.LOG.append(f"EDITOR SUMMARY (round {rnd}): {summary}")
    trace("edit_finished", round=rnd, summary=summary,
          timeline=[{"cut": c["cut"], "seconds": c["seconds"], "start": c["start"]} for c in et._timeline])
    return {**state, "edit_summary": summary, "edit_rounds": rnd}

On re-editing (after a failed check) the timeline is emptied but et.LOG is not: a ===== EDIT ROUND 2 ===== heading is added and logging continues, so what was done in round one, how the check failed, and what changed in round two stay in one log.

The instructions (EDITOR_SYSTEM) do not say what order to use. They say: look before you decide, explain any departure from the storyboard, obey these constraints, summarize your decisions at the end. The order itself is decided by the agent, after looking at the material.

Follow what it did through the log The weakness of orchestration is that it is hard to see what happened. So the tools keep the log. Lines like add_to_timeline(cut=1, seconds=8, start=0.5) accumulate in order, and at the release decision (7.4.7) they are shown to you together with the agent's own summary. When the run ends they are written to EDIT_LOG.txt.

7.4.6The check

def check(state: State) -> State:
    problems = []
    out = vt.PROJECT_ROOT / "output.mp4"
    if not out.exists():
        problems.append("output.mp4 was not written: the editor did not finish the tool sequence")
    else:
        dur = vt.probe_duration(out)
        if not (MIN_SECONDS <= dur <= MAX_SECONDS):
            problems.append(f"length is {dur:.1f}s, must be between {MIN_SECONDS} and {MAX_SECONDS}s")
        narr = vt.BUILD / "narration.mp3"
        if narr.exists() and vt.probe_duration(narr) > dur + 0.5:
            problems.append("narration is longer than the video: shorten the narration")
        subs_text = " ".join(et.LAST_SUBTITLES).lower()      # the subtitles the editor actually burned
        for statement, words in KEY_POINTS:
            if words.lower() not in subs_text:
                problems.append(f"no subtitle mentions '{words}' (key point: {statement})")
        unique_cuts = {c["cut"] for c in et._timeline}
        if len(unique_cuts) < MIN_CLIPS:
            problems.append(f"only {len(unique_cuts)} different clips were used; at least {MIN_CLIPS} are required")
        sources = vt.read_sources()
        for c in et._timeline:
            if c["cut"] not in sources:
                problems.append(f"cut{c['cut']} is on the timeline but has no line in assets/SOURCES.txt")
    text = "OK" if not problems else "\n".join("- " + p for p in problems)
    print("\nCHECK:", text)
    et.LOG.append("CHECK: " + ("PASSED" if not problems else "FAILED\n" + text))
    trace("check_completed", round=state.get("edit_rounds"), passed=not problems, problems=problems)
    return {**state, "check_output": text, "passed": not problems, "feedback": "" if not problems else text}

The check is a fixed procedure. Whatever the editing agent decided, it is measured here with the same ruler. The success conditions you added in 7.3.2 go into the same problems list, as sentences. Failures come out as sentences and go back to edit as feedback, up to MAX_ROUNDS times.

CheckWhat the editing agent does on failure
output.mp4 existsRedo the tool sequence
Length between 30 and 60 sTrim or extend clips
At least four different clips usedAdd an unused cut; if none exist, the human adds material
Narration no longer than the videoCut words, then regenerate the audio
Key-point words in the subtitlesFix the subtitles
A source line for every cut usedOnly a human can fix this: write it in SOURCES.txt

7.4.7Run it

python agent/agent_video.py

draft takes some tens of seconds at first (waiting for the 27B). After you place the clips and type done, you see the editing agent calling tools as [tool] … lines. At the end, output.mp4, the agent's summary of its decisions and the tool log are shown, and the run stops for the release decision. That decision is approve or stop only; there is no way back to the storyboard from here.

When the run ends you have EDIT_LOG.txt (tool calls and check results for every round) and run/EXECUTION_TRACE.jsonl (everything that happened, in order). These two are the "production record" of 7.6. On a re-run, the previous contents of run/ move to run/previous_<time>/ and a new record starts.

Use revise at least once For this exercise, use revise at least once at storyboard approval, to verify with your own eyes that the workflow can return to the planning node. Give one concrete improvement, even if the first storyboard is acceptable. This is not about finding fault; it is a test of the route back. The revision and its reason land in run/EXECUTION_TRACE.jsonl and give you item 8 of CHANGE_REPORT.txt.

7.5Why you still cannot publish

output.mp4 exists. The check passed. You still cannot publish it as it is. The check only looked at shape.

What a human confirms before releaseWhy a machine cannot
Are the facts in the narration right (numbers, places, history)?The 27B writes facts that sound right. It holds no evidence
Are the sources real, and within the terms?The check can see that a line exists, not whether it is true
Would someone who lives in Mumbai feel uncomfortable watching it?The prejudice on the flip side of "charm" is invisible to a machine
Do the key points work as a reason to come?A word in a subtitle is not the same as a message that lands

Confirm these four, then press approve. That is the producer's job. What the agent did was write, look at the material and join it, and measure. You handed the joining decisions to the agent, but they are in the log, they were measured by the check, and you looked last.
