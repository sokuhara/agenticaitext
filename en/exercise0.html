Exercise 0

Find work worth handing to an AI agent

You have just read what Agentic AI means: give it a goal, it plans the steps, uses tools, and decides what to do next from the result. This exercise asks you to apply that idea to real work, rather than memorise the definition.

01The task

No code yet. Spend 20 minutes thinking on your own.

What this task is not You are not being asked to research how these countries actually work. You assume a plausible workflow for a place, and then design how much of it an agent could take.

What is being tested is not country knowledge. It is whether you can spot repetition, multiple steps and checkpoints.

Pick one subject from the list. Then think about it for the United States, India and Kenya. You may use a subject of your own instead.

  • First-pass check of scholarship applications
  • Routing hospital appointments
  • Confirming crop collection and delivery
  • Checking university assignment submissions
  • Finding missing items in government paperwork

For your subject, name one piece of work per country that meets all of these.

  • The same shape of work happens again and again
  • It takes several steps, not one action
  • People do most of it today
  • If it goes wrong, someone has to answer for it
What this exercise checks "Repetitive." "Multi-step." "Currently done by people." Copying those words back is not enough.

Be specific. When does the work start? Who checks what? If it goes wrong at that point, who is affected?

03One rule

You will not know all three countries equally well. Where you do not know, write what you think is likely rather than looking it up. The point is not accuracy about the country.

But mark those parts. Write *guess next to anything you filled in that way, and [assumption: ...] for a condition you had to assume.

Why mark it The marking is practice at making your own assumptions visible to yourself.

One of the three countries is probably one you know almost nothing about. The table will still fill up, because it is easy to write something plausible. What matters is that afterwards you can tell which parts were knowledge and which were assumption.

Keep this sheet. You will open it again in Part 1, and once more in Part 6.

There is no single right answer here. Two questions are worth carrying into the rest of the course.

  • What kind of work becomes a candidate for delegation?
  • If you delegate it, who is affected, and how?

The second question is the one people skip.
